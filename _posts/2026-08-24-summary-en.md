## [AI writing traces found in ~90% of biomedical papers by late 2025](https://www.solidot.org/story?sid=85173) ⭐️ 8.0/10

An arXiv preprint analyzing PubMed Central papers found detectable LLM-assisted writing traces in 77% of 2025 English-language biomedical papers, up from 52% in 2024, reaching nearly 90% by December 2025. This aligns with a 2025 survey in which 71% of researchers self-reported using AI writing assistance. AI-assisted writing has shifted from a minority to the dominant mode of drafting biomedical papers, affecting researchers, journals, and peer reviewers. The finding raises questions about the integrity and transparency of scientific literature as AI contributions become pervasive. The detection method identifies stylistic traces of LLM use rather than confirming actual AI involvement, and the preprint has not yet been peer-reviewed. AI-assisted writing traces were more common in abstracts, introductions, and discussions than in methods and results sections.

rss · Solidot · Aug 24, 07:26

**Background**: arXiv is an open-access preprint repository where researchers share papers before peer review, while PubMed Central \(PMC\) is a free full-text archive of biomedical and life sciences journal literature maintained by the U.S. National Institutes of Health. The preprint analyzed English-language papers in PMC to estimate how often large language models \(LLMs\) such as GPT-4 are used in drafting scientific manuscripts.

## [Pew Study: Over a Third of English Webpages Show AI Traces](https://www.solidot.org/story?sid=85172) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Pew Research Center data scientists analyzed 500,000 English webpages over five years using AI-detection tools. They estimate that in a July 2026 snapshot, more than one-third of pages published after ChatGPT&\#x27;s late-2022 launch show detectable AI traces, up from roughly one in ten overall. This shows AI writing tools have become a structural force in English web publishing, not a marginal experiment. It affects publishers, readers, search engines, and content platforms, which must adapt to a web where AI-generated text is pervasive. AI text distribution varies by top-level domain: in the 2026 sample, one in ten .com pages showed AI traces, double the .org rate \(4.6%\) and ten times the .edu/.gov rate \(1%\). Compared with 2023, pages now use dashes twice as often, Oxford commas 63% more often, AI-favored words such as &\#x27;delve,&\#x27; &\#x27;interplay,&\#x27; and &\#x27;testament&\#x27; more than twice as often, and the parallel pattern &\#x27;it&\#x27;s not just X, it&\#x27;s Y&\#x27; nearly three times as often.

rss · Solidot · Aug 24, 07:06

**Background**: AI content detectors scan text and use machine learning to judge whether it was written by a human or generated by tools such as ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini. They often rely on statistical patterns and linguistic markers, such as repetitive sentence structures, overly uniform transitions, and certain word choices. The Pew analysis used such tools on a large sample of English webpages, though AI-detection accuracy is imperfect and the sample is limited to English-language pages.

The agency had terminated the institute&\#x27;s third-party authorization on July 22, 2026, and said the official warning magnitude was 5.4. ## [China Bans Primary-School Teaching in Kindergartens, Written Homework in Grade 1](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/4ybbiHCGKDjljk_NLhENLA) ⭐️ 8.0/10

China&\#x27;s Ministry of Education issued a Q&amp;A clarifying that kindergartens must not teach primary-school content such as pinyin, literacy, or arithmetic. It also mandates no written homework or paper exams in first grade and sets adaptation periods for primary and junior-high school students. This clarifies the regulatory boundary for early childhood and primary education, affecting kindergartens, schools, teachers, students, parents, and local education authorities nationwide. It reinforces the Preschool Education Law and may reshape expectations about homework and exam pressure in the first year of schooling. The first semester of first grade is designated as an adaptation period, with pinyin instruction guaranteed at least six to eight weeks. Junior-high schools may slow the teaching pace in the first month of the new semester to help students adjust.
