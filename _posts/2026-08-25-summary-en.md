## [FDA Authorizes First Wearable Device Monitoring Both Ketones and Blood Sugar](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-authorizes-first-wearable-device-continuously-monitors-both-ketone-levels-and-blood-sugar) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The FDA has authorized the first wearable device that continuously monitors both ketone levels and blood sugar, creating a new regulated product category through the De Novo classification pathway. This authorization establishes a new market-access boundary for continuous metabolic monitoring devices. This decision matters because it opens a new regulatory category for devices that continuously track multiple metabolic markers, potentially benefiting people with diabetes and metabolic conditions. It also signals a shift in how wearable metabolic monitors can reach the market, affecting manufacturers, clinicians, and payers. The authorization was issued under the FDA&\#x27;s De Novo pathway, which applies to novel low-to-moderate risk devices without a legally marketed predicate. The wearable sensor reads analytes in interstitial fluid to continuously measure glucose and ketone levels, and the new classification will govern future devices in this category.

hackernews · sunnynagra · Aug 25, 19:07 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49439017)

**Background**: Continuous glucose monitors \(CGMs\) have long been used to track blood sugar in people with diabetes, but ketone monitoring has traditionally relied on fingerstick blood tests or urine strips. Continuous ketone monitoring uses enzyme-based electrochemical sensors in interstitial fluid, similar to CGM technology. The FDA&\#x27;s De Novo pathway provides a route to classify novel devices that have no predicate, which is why this authorization creates a new product category.

## [Protocol Labs Cuts Funding for IPFS Core Team Shipyard](https://www.solidot.org/story?sid=85184) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Protocol Labs has withdrawn funding from Shipyard, the core maintenance team for IPFS, with IPFS-related work scheduled to end on September 30, 2026. This follows earlier exits by Cloudflare and Brave from supporting IPFS. The funding withdrawal raises serious questions about the long-term continuity of IPFS, a foundational decentralized storage protocol. Projects that depend on IPFS, such as Bluesky&\#x27;s AT Protocol, could be affected by reduced maintenance and development capacity. Shipyard stated that work related to IPFS will end on September 30, 2026. IPFS is a content-addressed peer-to-peer hypermedia distribution protocol developed by Protocol Labs since 2014, and Bluesky&\#x27;s AT Protocol is built on IPFS content addressing.

rss · Solidot · Aug 25, 04:53

**Background**: IPFS \(InterPlanetary File System\) is an open-source protocol for distributed file storage, sharing, and persistence, using content addressing to identify files by their content rather than location. It was created by Juan Benet at Protocol Labs in 2015 and relies on concepts such as distributed hash tables and MerkleDAG. Content-addressed storage allows data to be retrieved based on its content, which is a key idea behind both IPFS and the AT Protocol used by Bluesky. The withdrawal of Protocol Labs funding leaves IPFS without a clearly identified core maintenance team after 2026.
