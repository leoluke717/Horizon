## [Shanghai&\#x27;s &\#x27;Hu Eight Measures&\#x27; Take Effect to Revive Housing Demand](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cj/2026/08-23/10682703.shtml) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Shanghai officially implemented the &\#x27;Hu Eight Measures&\#x27; \(沪八条\) housing policy package on August 21, covering five areas and eight measures. The first weekend after implementation saw a visible response in the new-home market, with reporters visiting two improvement-type housing projects. The policy package changes the rules for home buyers, sellers, developers, and banks, potentially lowering transaction costs and stimulating demand. However, the scale and durability of the market impact remain uncertain because comprehensive transaction data are not yet available. The eight measures include optimizing provident fund withdrawal, optimizing personal housing credit, implementing trade-in purchase subsidies, promoting housing ticket resettlement, and advancing government purchase of second-hand homes. The first-weekend response was strongest in the new-home market, particularly at two improvement-type projects inside and outside the outer ring road.

**Background**: Shanghai&\#x27;s real estate market has faced cooling demand, and local governments often use policy packages to stabilize transactions. &\#x27;Housing ticket resettlement&\#x27; refers to compensating residents with vouchers that can be used to buy new homes, while &\#x27;trade-in&\#x27; subsidies encourage households to sell old homes and buy new ones. The &\#x27;outer ring road&\#x27; distinction in Shanghai roughly separates more central areas from outer districts, which have different price levels and demand patterns.

## [Ulanqab Becomes China&\#x27;s AI Data-Center Hub With 12.5 GW Commitments](https://www.wired.com/story/the-unlikely-place-at-the-center-of-chinas-ai-boom/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Goldman Sachs research cited by WIRED shows that nearly 100 data centers have opened or broken ground in Ulanqab, Inner Mongolia, since 2016, with committed capacity reaching 12.5 GW. More than 70% of that capacity was announced in the past year, exceeding OpenAI&\#x27;s Stargate plan of 10 GW. This marks a major capital-allocation shift in AI infrastructure, with Chinese tech giants such as DeepSeek, ByteDance, Alibaba, and Xiaohongshu building their own data centers in a single inland city. It shows how China is rapidly scaling AI compute outside traditional coastal hubs, with implications for the regional economy, power grid, and water resources. Ulanqab&\#x27;s appeal comes from its cold climate, low electricity prices, and proximity to Beijing, but water scarcity is a concern: annual precipitation is only about 14 inches, and last month a local water plant had to shut off supply for seven hours each night. About 37% of local electricity still comes from coal, and committed capacity may not all be built.

**Background**: Ulanqab is a prefecture-level city in Inner Mongolia, China, that has become a favored location for data centers because of its cool climate, cheap land and power, and fiber-optic links to Beijing. The Stargate Project is a U.S. joint venture by OpenAI, SoftBank, Oracle, and MGX that plans to invest up to $500 billion in AI infrastructure by 2029. DeepSeek is a Chinese AI company known for cost-efficient open-weight models, and it is among the firms building data centers in Ulanqab.

## [Microsoft&\#x27;s new Windows 11 app forces Bing as default search in Chrome, Firefox, Brave](https://www.windowslatest.com/2026/08/22/microsoft-built-a-dedicated-app-that-forces-bing-everywhere-on-windows-11-including-chrome-firefox-and-brave/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Microsoft has deployed a dedicated Windows 11 app called Microsoft Recommended Search Settings that changes the default search engine in Chrome, Firefox, and Brave to Bing. The app is hosted on Microsoft&\#x27;s official servers rather than distributed through Windows Update or the Microsoft Store, and it redirects users to Microsoft Rewards after installation. This extends Microsoft&\#x27;s search distribution leverage beyond its own Edge browser, directly competing with Google, Mozilla, and Brave over default search settings. It could affect Windows 11 users&\#x27; browsing experience and intensify antitrust scrutiny over how default choices are changed on the operating system. Hands-on testing showed Chrome prompting users to switch back to Google, while Microsoft displays a &quot;wait, don&\#x27;t switch back&quot; message to retain them. The associated Bing extension reportedly has 5 million users, and the app&\#x27;s installation scope and whether it is opt-in or forced remain unclear.
